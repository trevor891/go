1

Your name

Google “corrects” your name to someone else’s.

On November 11 I searched “Bayside Gulf Carts.” Google rewrote it to “Bayside Golf Carts” and listed Bay City Carts in Tampa and Bayside Golf Cart Rentals in Bay St. Louis, Mississippi. Neither is you. Your website calls you “Gulf Carts,” and your Facebook page lists 859-353-2027 while your site lists 850-353-2027 (likely a typo on one of them).

Your name, rewritten
Not you
The fix: pick one name and use it on your ad, Facebook page, website and Google Business Profile, fix the phone number on Facebook, and link the website from the page. Shoppers who search what the ad told them should land on you.

Google search “Bayside Gulf Carts,” November 11, 2026. My searches were not run from your area, so the map results a local shopper sees may differ; the name rewrite is the point.

2

Your ads

The people who do find your site can’t be shown your carts again.

Your ad’s two cards (“Shop Your Next Golf Cart” and “Schedule Service Today”) open Facebook with a Get Quote button. Your site has inventory with prices, but when I loaded gulfcartscustom.com, scrolled and waited, the Google tag fired and no Meta pixel did.

Step 1A Sandestin shopper sees your ad
Step 2They look you up and browse your inventory on gulfcartscustom.com
Step 3They leave. With no Meta pixel, Facebook can’t show them that cart again
The fix: add the Meta pixel to your site (about 15 minutes), build a retargeting audience of everyone who viewed a cart, financing or contact page, and let Facebook learn who actually buys so it can find more people like them.
