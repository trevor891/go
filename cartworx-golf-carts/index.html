The right-hand panel is what Google treats as "the answer" for a business search. I couldn't find a Google Business Profile for Cartworx, so Google fills that space with the nearest thing it knows: a local golf course.

The fix: create and verify a Google Business Profile at 1209 E State Rd 2 (with "behind Fitness Plus Gym" in the directions), category "Golf cart dealer," plus hours, photos of your custom builds, and your phone number.

None of these are in La Porte

Google search on November 2, 2026. Results vary by searcher and location.

Google's top three for "golf carts la porte indiana" are all out of town: Carts on 12 (4.4 stars, 75 reviews), Quik Carts (5.0, 86) and Tigon (4.8, 64). You're the dealer that's actually in La Porte, and you're not on the list.

The fix: once the profile is live, ask every buyer and service customer for a Google review (a text with the link, the day they pick up). Your Facebook page already shows a 5.0 rating, so the happy customers exist. Getting them onto Google is what moves you into that top three.
3

Your ad

Your ad has one door, and it closes at 5

Your ad says "Your golf cart shouldn't look like everyone else's" and the only button is Call now. Someone scrolling at 9 p.m. who wants to see your builds, check prices, or ask about a custom job has nowhere to go but your Facebook page.

There's a second cost. Without a website there's no Meta pixel, so Facebook can't show your ad again to the people who were interested, and it can't learn what a Cartworx buyer looks like.

The fix: a simple one-page site with your current inventory, custom build photos, and a "tell me what you want built" form, with the Meta pixel installed. Then point the ad at that page so every click is a lead you can follow up with.
